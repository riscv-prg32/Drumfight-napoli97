# Audio

Drumfight uses the PRG32 SID-like procedural synthesizer and nothing else:
no PCM samples, no tracker music. The cartridge **is** the sequencer.

## Eight voices, eight channels

The firmware mixes eight synth channels (`CONFIG_PRG32_AUDIO_MAX_VOICES=8`
in both the ESP32-C6 and the QEMU profile). Voice *n* of the pattern always
plays instrument *n* on channel *n*, so the whole kit can sound on one step
and a new hit of a voice cuts only its own previous hit, as on a real drum
machine.

The kit is generated by [`tools/build_audio.py`](../tools/build_audio.py)
into [`audio/audio.json`](../audio/audio.json) and packed into the
cartridge's AUD0 block (104 bytes: eight 8-byte descriptors and a header).

| # | Voice | Waveform | Cutoff | Res. | Decay | Pan | MIDI note | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | KICK | triangle | 8 | 2 | 60 | 0 | 36 | bass drum, centre |
| 1 | SNARE | noise | 15 | 1 | 46 | -14 | 106 | snare, slightly left |
| 2 | HAT | noise | 15 | 3 | 16 | +30 | 127 | closed hat, right |
| 3 | OPEN HAT | noise | 15 | 3 | 74 | +40 | 124 | open hat, right |
| 4 | TAMMORRA | triangle | 11 | 2 | 64 | -40 | 45 | Campanian frame drum, left |
| 5 | BONGO | pulse (width 4) | 12 | 2 | 34 | +20 | 64 | high hand drum |
| 6 | CAMPANA | pulse (width 6) | 15 | 3 | 36 | -28 | 81 | cowbell |
| 7 | CLAP | noise | 13 | 2 | 34 | +12 | 112 | hand clap |

- Attack is 0 and sustain is 0 for every voice: a percussive envelope that
  decays to silence. The decay byte maps to `1 + d*d*2000/65025` ms (decay
  60 is about 110 ms).
- For noise voices the MIDI note is the clock of the noise generator: a
  higher note is a brighter noise.
- The synthesizer's low-pass filter tops out at about 1.3 kHz (cutoff 15),
  so hats are darker than on a sampled kit. This is the character of the
  platform, not a bug.
- The MIDI notes and per-voice gains live in `VOICE_NOTES` / `VOICE_GAIN` in
  [`src/drumfight.c`](../src/drumfight.c); the rest is in the instrument
  descriptors.

## Stereo

Pan runs from -64 (left) to +63 (right) and is stored as the default pan of
each instrument; the cartridge passes "centre", which tells the firmware to
use that default. On stereo hardware (PRG32 Audio Plus, two MAX98357A
boards) the kit is spread like a drum circle; on mono hardware and in QEMU
(mono UART stream) the firmware collapses it, with no change to the game.

## Dynamics

| Level | Note-on volume |
|---|---|
| ghost | 112 |
| hit | 188 |
| accent | 255 |

each scaled by the gain of the voice. The mixer applies the note volume
twice (voice and channel), so loudness follows the square: a ghost note is
about 19 % of an accent, a hit about 54 %. The title screen plays at two
thirds of these values.

## Sequencer clock

The firmware calls the cartridge every 33 ms. Steps are scheduled on an
exact clock: each frame adds `elapsed_ms * bpm` to an accumulator and a
sixteenth fires every 15,000 units (`STEP_UNITS`). The tempo therefore
never drifts (`tests/test_game.c` checks 8 bars at 112 BPM to within 40 ms),
but a step sounds on the first frame after its ideal time: up to 33 ms late,
with the jitter that implies. Live hits sound immediately.

A hit recorded early (assigned to the next step) is marked so the sequencer
does not play it a second time when that step arrives.

## Other sounds

Menu blips, the unlock jingle, the move flourish and the final fanfare are
short note sequences on the campana, bongo and kick channels, played through
a small frame-delay queue (`jingle()`), with the instrument's pitch changed
by the note number. While the bar is empty a quiet campana metronome marks
the beats.

## What has and has not been checked

The QEMU capture of the preview script has a peak of -13 dBFS, no clipped
samples and an RMS that rises as the groove builds. Nobody has yet listened
to the kit on a physical speaker: balance and timbre of the eight voices are
an open acceptance item ([testing.md](testing.md)), and the table above is
where to tune them.
